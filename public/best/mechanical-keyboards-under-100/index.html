Peripherals guide

Best mechanical keyboards under $100

The Keychron V-series (V1 or V3 in QMK trim) is our top pick for the best all-around mechanical keyboard under $100 in 2026, thanks to its hot-swap sockets, gasket-mounted build, and genuinely good out-of-box sound.

Updated June 2026 · 6 picks compared · every weakness listed

Sub-$100 mechanical keyboards have gotten genuinely good, but the marketing has gotten louder too. A lot of boards in this range lean on buzzwords like "gasket mount" and "south-facing PCB" while shipping with rattly stabilizers or QC that varies unit to unit. Below are the boards we think actually hold up, with the real trade-offs spelled out. Note that prices swing with sales and switch choice, and the wireless models almost always cost more than their wired siblings.

Straight answers

Questions worth asking first

How does Frank make money?+

We earn affiliate commissions when you buy through our links, mostly via Amazon Associates. That commission never changes how we rank a product. The picks and trust scores are decided before any link goes in, and we keep cons on every board even when it might cost us a sale.

Is hot-swap actually worth it under $100?+

Yes, if you think you might ever change switches. Hot-swap lets you pull and replace switches by hand with no soldering, which is the easiest way to fix a scratchy feel or change your sound later. Most boards on this list include it. If you are certain you will never tinker, a non-hot-swap board can save a few dollars, but the resale and longevity argument favors hot-swap.

Do I need QMK/VIA, or is the included software fine?+

QMK/VIA gives you deep, offline remapping that lives on the keyboard itself, which is why we weight it heavily. Brand software from Royal Kludge, Epomaker, and Glorious works but tends to be clunkier and sometimes cloud-dependent. If you remap keys or build layers often, QMK/VIA (Keychron V and Pro lines) is the safer bet. For plug-and-type users, the bundled software is usually fine.

Why do the wireless versions cost more than advertised?+

Most of these brands sell a cheaper wired model and a pricier triple-mode wireless model under similar names. Adding Bluetooth and a 2.4GHz dongle, plus hot-swap, pushes the price up. Boards like the NuPhy Air V2 and Keychron K8 Pro often hover right at or just over $100 once you pick the wireless, hot-swap configuration, so check the exact variant before buying.
