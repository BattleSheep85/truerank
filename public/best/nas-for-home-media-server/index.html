Best NAS for a home media server

For most home media servers in 2026 the UGREEN NASync DXP4800 Plus is the top pick, with a quad-core x86 chip and Intel quick sync that handles Plex and Jellyfin transcoding far better than anything else near its price.

Updated June 2026 · 6 picks compared · every weakness listed

Picking a NAS for a home media server comes down to one question most buying guides dodge: can the box actually transcode your library, or are you betting your whole setup on direct play. We weighted real transcoding capability (Intel Quick Sync versus ARM versus AMD), software maturity, noise, and price, and we were honest about the tradeoffs. The cheap ARM boxes look great on a spec sheet until you point a 4K HDR file at a phone and watch it choke. The polished ecosystems (Synology) charge a premium and have been quietly locking down drive compatibility. There is no single right answer, so we ranked by who each box is actually for.

Straight answers

Questions worth asking first

Do I actually need hardware transcoding for Plex or Jellyfin?+

Only if your player and your file do not match. If every device direct-plays your files (same codec, container, and resolution they support), even a weak NAS is fine. The moment you stream a 4K HDR file to a phone, a browser, or over limited bandwidth, the server has to transcode. That is when an Intel Quick Sync iGPU (UGREEN, QNAP, Asustor, TerraMaster) matters and a chip without an iGPU (Synology DS923+) struggles. Be honest about your devices before deciding.

Why is Synology ranked behind cheaper boxes if its software is the best?+

Because this is a media-server guide, and transcoding is where Synology has gone backwards. Recent DSM versions dropped or limited hardware video transcoding, and the chips Synology ships are weak for it. The software is still the best in the business for backups, photos, and reliability, which is why the DS224+ still earns a high software score. But if your priority is Plex or Jellyfin transcoding, you are paying a premium for the wrong strengths.

Should I buy the NAS with drives included or diskless?+

Buy diskless and add your own drives. Prices here are diskless because that is how these are mostly sold and how you should buy them. Pick drives separately based on capacity and warranty (CMR drives, not SMR, for a NAS). One caution: Synology has been steering buyers toward its own branded drives through compatibility lists, so check the current list before committing if you go Synology.
