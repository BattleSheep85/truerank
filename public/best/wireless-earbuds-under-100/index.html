Best wireless earbuds under $100

The Soundcore Liberty 5 from Anker is our top pick for the best all-round wireless earbuds under $100, balancing strong ANC, solid battery, and tuneable sound better than anything else in the price bracket.

Updated June 2026 · 6 picks compared · every weakness listed

Sub-$100 earbuds have gotten genuinely good, but the category is messy. Some chase flashy specs they cannot back up, others nail sound but skip noise cancelling or a usable mic. We focused on models you can actually buy in 2026 from Soundcore/Anker, Nothing, JLab, EarFun, Samsung, and Google, and we weighed the things that matter day to day: how they sound out of the box, whether ANC does anything real, comfort over long sessions, honest battery life, and call quality. No pick here is perfect, so every one gets at least one con worth knowing before you spend.

Is active noise cancelling worth it under $100?+

It depends on where you use the earbuds. Budget ANC will not match a flagship pair, but models like the Soundcore Liberty 5, EarFun Air Pro 4, and Nothing Ear (a) cut low rumble from planes, buses, and HVAC noise meaningfully. If you mostly listen in quiet rooms, you can save money and skip it, like the Pixel Buds A-Series do.

Do these earbuds work with both iPhone and Android?+

Yes, all of them pair over standard Bluetooth and work with either platform. The catch is companion apps and extra features. Samsung's deepest features favor Galaxy phones, Google's favor Pixel and Android, and the rest rely on their own apps that run on both iOS and Android. Core listening and calls work everywhere.

Which pick is best if I take a lot of calls?+

For call clarity in everyday conditions, the Google Pixel Buds A-Series are the most consistent in this group, with the Soundcore Liberty 5 close behind. If you regularly take calls in loud places, none of these match a premium pair, so manage expectations and consider a model with multiple beamforming mics.
